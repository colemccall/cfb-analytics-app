How Ratings Work

A plain-English explanation of the EDGE rating system — formula, inputs, calibration, and known limitations.

The Core Idea

CFB recruiting rankings measure potential — scout projections before a player has played a college snap. Our ratings measure production — what a player actually did on the field, adjusted for the quality of competition they faced.

A 3-star receiver who dominates in a Power 4 conference will rate higher than a 5-star recruit who hasn't produced. That gap — production vs. hype — is what this platform is designed to find.

Ratings run from 30 to 99. They are absolute scores, not relative rankings — the same production produces the same rating regardless of how many other players had a great year. A 90-rated QB in 2023 met the same performance threshold as a 90-rated QB in 2025.

The EDGE Formula

EDGE (Efficiency-Driven Grade per Event) is the core metric behind every rating. It answers: "How much did this player produce, game by game, against the quality of defense or offense they faced?"

EDGE = Σ (stat_compositei × opp_multi) / √(games_played) Where i = each game the player appeared in with recorded stats

Step 1 — Stat Composite (per game)

Each position has a position-specific weighted formula applied to that game's stats. Weights are calibrated to prioritize impactful plays — touchdowns, turnovers, disruption — over volume-based counting stats.

Example weights: QB composite = passYds×1.0 + passTD×25 + rushYds×0.7 + rushTD×20 − INTs×20. A QB who throws 4 TDs in one game scores much higher than one with the same total yards but no touchdowns.

Step 2 — Opponent Quality Multiplier

Each game's composite is scaled by the opponent's SP+ rating for the relevant side of the ball: defensive SP+ for offensive players, offensive SP+ for defensive players. The multiplier is normalized per game to a range of [0.55, 1.45] — a great performance against a top-10 defense counts up to 1.45×, while the same production against a weak defense counts as low as 0.55×.

This is why scheme matters. A QB in an air-raid offense facing MAC defenses will not rate the same as one producing equal totals against SEC and Big Ten defenses — even if their raw stat lines look identical.

Step 3 — Sample Size Normalization

The sum of opponent-adjusted game composites is divided by √(games played). This rewards production over many games without catastrophically penalizing players who miss games to injury. A player who dominates for 14 games rates higher than one with one great game.

Each position has a minimum stats_measured threshold — the season total of primary countable stats (e.g., passing attempts for QBs, tackles + disruption plays for defenders). Players below the threshold are treated as non-qualifiers and receive recruiting-blended estimates instead of pure production ratings.

EDGE → Overall Rating

EDGE scores map to Overall Ratings (OVR) through fixed position-specific piecewise linear anchors. The anchors are calibrated from known reference players — elite All-American seasons produce 95+, average starters land in the 65–75 range, and backups with minimal production land around 50.

This is a rating system, not a ranking system. The thresholds don't change year to year. If no one in the 2024 class hit the 99 threshold, no one rates 99 — there's no forced distribution curve.

Why are raw tackles weighted so low for defenders? Tackles are credited based on proximity, not necessarily impact — a linebacker being "in the right spot" on a 4-yard run differs enormously from one making a tackle for loss. TFLs (behind the line of scrimmage), sacks, and hurries are genuine disruption plays with meaningful weight. Tackles are included but given a fraction of the weight to avoid rewarding volume over impact.

Playing-Time Tiers

Formulas are only reliable with enough volume — a QB with 15 passing attempts has a meaningless yards-per-attempt figure. Rather than a binary cutoff, we use four tiers that blend production stats and recruiting composite differently based on how much a player recorded:

TierFormula treatmentWhat it means
Starter100% EDGE-based OVRFull season contributor — stats drive the rating
Role player75% EDGE + 25% recruitingRegular contributor, limited sample; stats partially trusted
Reserve40% EDGE + 60% recruitingSpot duty; recruiting anchors more heavily
Bench / Redshirt100% recruiting compositeNo qualifying production to evaluate yet

There are no artificial caps on any tier. A highly-recruited backup at a major program can still rate well — the tier controls how much we trust the stats, not the rating ceiling.

Trajectory and Breakout

Trajectory (▲▼) is the change in overall rating from the prior season — positive = improved production relative to the same position threshold.

Breakout candidate 🔥 flags players who are young (FR/SO), have a high recruiting composite (>70/100), but are currently rated below the positional median — suggesting upside not yet reflected in production. This is a heuristic, not a model prediction.

Known Limitations

OL — Stat Deficit Across the Board

No individual offensive lineman statistics exist in any publicly available college football data source. There are no personal blocking grades, pressure rates allowed, or run-block efficiency numbers tracked at the player level. As a result, every OL rating is a team-level proxy — rush yards per attempt and sack rate allowed are averaged across the entire offensive line and distributed evenly. An elite LT and a mediocre RT at the same school will receive identical ratings. This is a structural limitation of the data ecosystem, not a modeling choice.

Pre-2016 Defense — Severely Stat-Limited

The CFB Data API does not include individual defensive stats — tackles, sacks, TFLs, QB hurries, or PBUs — for seasons prior to 2016. This is a hard API limitation, not a data gap we can fill from our pipeline. The impact varies significantly by position group:

  • CB / S / DB (moderate impact): Interceptions are available for all seasons and carry meaningful signal — a 4-INT corner is correctly identified as an above-average player. The pre-2016 CLASSIC system blends INTs with recruiting composite using adaptive weighting (more INTs = more weight on production, fewer INTs = more recruiting anchor). Ratings are directionally correct but lack the PBU and tackle context that would sharpen precision.
  • LB (significant impact): Interceptions are available but are rare for linebackers — the position's most valuable stats (tackle-for-loss, sacks, pressures) are missing entirely. Pre-2016 LB ratings lean heavily on recruiting composite as a proxy for player quality, which systematically underrates productive lower-recruited players and overrates high-recruited players who underperformed.
  • DL / EDGE (severe impact): These positions have essentially no useful pre-2016 individual stats — DL players almost never record interceptions, and their core stats (sacks, hurries, TFLs) are entirely absent from the API. Pre-2016 DL and EDGE ratings are capped based on recruiting composite (max ~88 for EDGE, ~85 for DL), regardless of actual production. A dominant defensive end like Myles Garrett in 2015 cannot be fully rated — his rating reflects recruiting ceiling, not what he did on the field.

What this means when reading pre-2016 defensive ratings: DB and LB ratings are usable but imprecise. DL and EDGE ratings prior to 2016 should be treated as recruiting-caliber estimates, not production ratings — they tell you the player's ceiling as projected by scouts, not what they actually did. A planned scraper for Sports Reference historical data would resolve this for seasons 2008–2015.

Additional model limitations

  • Coverage snaps for CB/S: We count INTs and PBUs, not targets allowed or coverage yards. A lockdown corner who is never thrown at will show low counting stats and rate below their true impact.
  • Tackle quality beyond TFLs: We minimize raw tackle weight for this reason, but can't distinguish a broken-tackle stop from a gang-tackle pile-on with existing data.
  • Receiver targets for WR/TE: EDGE reflects production per game, but can't distinguish a receiver who ran perfect routes but was ignored by the QB from one who was targeted and dropped passes.
  • Transfer Year 1 context: The model doesn't yet adjust for first-year transfer learning curves. Research question RQ9 (Year 1 vs. Year 2 transfer performance) is planned.
  • Games missed due to injury: The √(games_played) normalization reduces the penalty for missing games, but a player who misses half a season still has a lower cumulative EDGE than a healthy peer with equal per-game output.

Team Ratings

Team overall ratings blend four signals: SP+ (schedule-adjusted performance), roster quality (average EDGE rating of starter-tier players), recruiting pipeline (5-year rolling composite average), and a small stability anchor toward the national mean to prevent teams with missing data from collapsing to zero.

Team OVR = 45% SP+ + 35% Roster Quality + 15% Recruiting + 5% Anchor (50) Offense/Defense splits use 50% SP+ + 45% side-specific roster avg + 5% recruiting

SP+ Scaling

SP+ is a raw number centered near 0 (national average). It is passed through a sigmoid function scaled to the season's mean and standard deviation, mapping the full SP+ range to 0–100. An SP+ of roughly +30 (historically elite) maps to ~85; −30 (basement) maps to ~15; the national mean maps to ~50.

Roster Quality

Only starter-tier players contribute — players who crossed the position-specific minimum-snap threshold in script 06. Offensive and defensive averages are computed separately from each side's position groups. This means a team's team offense rating reflects actual offensive starter quality, not the full roster.

Recruiting

A 5-year rolling average of the 247Sports composite score for all players who signed with the team in that window. Scaled from the realistic range (roughly 0.80–1.00 composite) to 0–100. This rewards programs that consistently land top classes even before those players contribute production ratings.

Team ratings are currently available for 2025 only. Historical team ratings (2008–2024) require per-season runs of the team rating pipeline and are on the roadmap.
